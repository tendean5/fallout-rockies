# Fallout Rockies — Concept

> Working title. An original game in the spirit of *Fallout* — inspired by the
> *Fallout 1–2* tone, but not a clone and not Bethesda IP. Colorado-specific,
> Western frontier flavor, 1-player, roguelike, gritty with humor.

## Premise

A post-apocalyptic Colorado. A nuclear-era collapse — the cause never gets a
single agreed-upon answer, and no faction has the whole story. The player is an
**Enclave kid**, banished for *seeing* something in the airport bunker that the
Enclave was built to hide, and thrown into the wastes.

The game loops: **die and return.** Death pulls you back to the edge of the
wastes, carrying only artifacts, knowledge, and how factions see you — never a
full reset of the world, and never a guaranteed homecoming. The ending is a
choice, not a defeat condition.

## Tone

Gritty, but funny where it hurts. Never mustache-twirling. Western frontier
meets bunker-punk: sheriff vs. outlaw translated into a wasteland, but with
Colorado specificity everywhere — altitude, the wind, the snow, the light, the
river.

## The world (lore spine)

Denver and the high country are the two halves of the game — a ruined urban
grid contrasted with a frontier high-country. Each landmark doubles as a lore
and faction anchor.

### Urban (Denver / Denver-metro)
- **Denver airport bunkers (the Enclave)** — the last home, where the player
  was exiled from. Safety vs. control.
- **Blucifer statue** — a demonic statue in Denver that people worship.
  Unweathered in a world that rots everything else. The cult has a claim:
  it did the world a service. The player hunts the remains of the man who got
  crushed by it — the idol's story.
- **Stadiums & sports venues** (Broncos, others) — a refused sub-city of
  deserted lots; the stadiums could host a fan faction.
- **Red Rocks** — the famous amphitheater. The stage where factions rally,
  cults preach, and people are forced to look at each other.
- **Government buildings** — the old Denver sprawl, contested territory.
- **Denver Tech Center** — old research camps; the zone where "real" tech
  lives or used to.
- **Boulder** — the science town. University campus split down the middle: one
  side thrives on old-world research; the other is a poison zone. The kid
  wanders between. The library/archive is the densest repository of old-world
  knowledge, and possibly where the Enclave hid the record of the collapse.

### High country (Summit County + Colorado Springs)
- **Cheyenne Mountain** — the radar/early-warning complex. Survivors kept the
  dome lit by hand. The mystery: what it tracks (or whether it does at all).
- **NORAD** — the rival mountain. Second record of the same event — one lit,
  one dark; the player has to work out which story is true.
- **USAFA / Colorado Springs** — the military stronghold. Trained
  professionals with a moral code and a "mission" that may not be what it
  claims to be.
- **Lookout Mountain / high points** — lookout/observation sites; factions
  tracking each other from the top of the world.
- **Ski towns (Vail parody faction)** — a snow-elite faction, the
  "Springs-vs.-Snow" dynamic. Skiing as a luxury and a game.
- **Golden** — the western heart: **Coors brewery** (the old production
  plant, the wasteland's resource economy), **Colorado School of Mines**
  (the mines and the engineers), and **western-themed robots** — the mine
  drills and outlaw automata that no other wasteland could produce.
- **Mining ghost towns (Leadville)** — the Colorado frontier, wrecked by the
  collapse. Hard-edged, stubborn, frontier survival.

### River / low country
- **Platte River** — the mutants and new biology. New ecological world,
  creek-beds, the floodplain.

### Mutated animals
- **Moose** — the giant antlered threat.
- **Elk** — herds that split and changed behavior; the grazing faction.
- **Bighorn sheep** — the cliff creatures; the hard, cold survivors.
- **Trout** — the river fish, source and hunger.
- **Bugs** — the mass swarms, the clouds that roll over the floodplain.

## Factions (draft)

**Enclave** — the airport bunker stronghold; home, safety, and control.
Punished the kid for seeing the truth.
**The Faithful** — Blucifer cult; sincere devotion to a statue that may be a
scapegoat, a weapon, or a lie.
**USAFA / the Airmen** — trained professionals with a moral code. The closest
thing to the future the world has.
**The Snow Club (ski towns)** — a parody-elite of ski culture: snow, luxe,
and a frontier buried under a different kind of ice.
**Golden brewery & mines** — the western resource guild; the "gold rush"
legacy of a town named for gold.
**Boulder science faction** — the lab-keepers and the medics; the uneasy
transition between old and new.
**Platte River survivors** — the ecological faction; the first generation
born into the new world.
**Outlaws / free towns** — unaligned survivors; the frontier at loose
ends.

Each faction has: (a) something real to gain, (b) a genuinely human need,
(c) a nasty method. No faction is purely good or purely bad.

## Run structure (roguelike, Hades-style)

- One life per run. You die, you go back to the edge of the wastes.
- Carry forward: artifacts, what you learned, how factions actually see you.
  Never: full progression or a full world reset.
- Structured to make a **choice-based ending** sharp: the ending is a
  question, not a defeat state.

## Ending shape

No defeat states, no "correct" ending. The kid ends with a choice; each
epilogue is different but consistent:

- **Rebellion** — reclaim the Enclave / burn it.
- **Leave** — leave behind the world and choose a new one.
- **Join** — take a side, knowingly.
- **Burn it all** — end the war of the factions.

## Scope & status

Single-player, small team, one playthrough arc. Built to test the idea:
game feel + the "is it funny and sad at once?" question.
